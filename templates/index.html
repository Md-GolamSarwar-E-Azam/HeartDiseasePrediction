03 Heart test results
Use the category from the medical record, not a self-diagnosis.
What does this mean?

Choose the category documented in the medical record. These labels describe how the pain was classified during assessment; they are not a way to diagnose the cause of your own symptoms.

  • 0 — Typical angina: a chest discomfort pattern classified as typical angina.
  • 1 — Atypical angina: a pattern with some, but not all, features of typical angina.
  • 2 — Non-anginal pain: pain classified as not having the typical angina pattern.
  • 3 — Asymptomatic: no chest-pain symptoms reported for this assessment.

Angina is chest discomfort that can occur when the heart muscle does not get enough blood flow. A clinician must interpret its cause.

Resting electrocardiographic result.
What does this mean?

An ECG (electrocardiogram) records the heart’s electrical signals. Select the resting ECG category written in the report:

  • 0 — Normal / no notable finding: no finding represented by the other categories.
  • 1 — ST-T wave abnormality: a change noted in parts of the ECG tracing.
  • 2 — Left ventricular hypertrophy: an ECG category associated with increased muscle thickness in the heart’s main pumping chamber.

Only a health professional can interpret what an ECG finding means for an individual.

What does this mean?

A thallium stress test is a type of nuclear imaging test that helps clinicians assess blood flow to the heart muscle. Select the report’s category:

  • 1 or 3 — Normal: the dataset lists both codes as normal categories.
  • 6 — Fixed defect: a finding that appears persistent across parts of the test.
